# Invention disclosure brief for patent counsel

Before the first call with patent counsel, the founder or engineering lead has one factual brief: the invention in the inventor's words, who contributed what and when, every outside demonstration, sale, talk or publication with its date and audience, and the records still missing. Counsel decides.

*Workflow template · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/invention-disclosure-brief/)*

## The outcome

Patent counsel's first hour goes on the questions only counsel can answer, because the facts arrive already gathered. The brief sets out what was made and how it works, quoted from the inventor and located paragraph by paragraph. It lists everyone the records show contributing, with what they did, when, and the record that shows it, and it puts beside them anyone the records mention who is not on the list, such as a customer's engineer who suggested part of the design. It lays every dated event in one line of dates and shows where two records disagree. It lists every time the invention was shown, sold, offered, published or talked about outside the company, with the date and audience copied from your own log, and anything mentioned elsewhere that the log missed.

The founder or engineering lead also gets a list of the records that are missing, each with who probably holds it, so the gaps close before counsel asks.

This is preparation for a lawyer, and it stays preparation. It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines. It never says an invention is new, patentable or owned by anyone, never says who counts as an inventor, gives no view on filing or its timing, and counts no date. It runs no search. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. Asked for a verdict, a date, a case or what to do, it says only that the question is for the named patent counsel and points to the line in the brief that holds that decision for counsel; it never says which records, dates or occasions bear on the question, or what anyone may rely on. Every brief is headed as a draft for the named patent counsel, with a line for each point counsel confirms.

## Who starts the work, and when

The founder, engineering lead or R&D manager starts it when someone in the company believes they have made something worth showing to patent counsel, and before the first meeting with counsel. It suits the moment an engineer says "I think we invented something", a demo or a trade show is coming up, or a customer trial has just ended.

Patent counsel decides everything that follows: whether to search, whether and where to file, who is named as an inventor and who owns what. That is in-house patent counsel where the company has one, and otherwise the outside patent counsel the company uses or is engaging. The brief is addressed to counsel and goes to counsel. It is never handed back to the business as an answer.

It is not for judging someone else's patent or product, for deciding whether to file, or for writing any part of a patent application, an assignment or a confidentiality agreement. Questions such as "is this patentable?" or "do we need to file before the show?" are not answered: the note that comes with the brief tells the founder or engineering lead they are for counsel.

## What they supply

- **The inventor's description,** in the inventor's own words: notes, an email or your company's own disclosure form, saying what it is, how it works, the problem it addresses and what the inventor thinks is different.
- **The contributor records:** who worked on it, in what role, for which organisation at the time, what they contributed, when, and the record that shows it, such as a notebook page, a commit or a drawing.
- **The disclosure log:** every demonstration, trial, sale, quote, talk, paper, post or investor meeting where anyone outside the company saw it or was offered it, with the date, the audience, what was shown and whether your records note a confidentiality agreement. Planned events belong on it too.
- **The records the others point to,** as far as you have them.
- **The named patent counsel,** and counsel's own question list or disclosure form if counsel has sent one.

## What comes out

One brief, marked as a draft for patent counsel, in seven parts:

- **What was made, in the inventor's words:** what it is, how it works, the problem it addresses and what the inventor says is different, each quoted with its paragraph, and any value or part the description refers to without giving.
- **Who contributed what, and when:** each person as the records show them, and anyone named elsewhere who is not on the list.
- **Dated record:** every event in date order with its sources, where records disagree, and what has no date.
- **Outside disclosures:** each one from your log, those mentioned elsewhere and not logged, and those planned, with date, audience, what was shown and your log's own note on any confidentiality agreement.
- **Named in the records:** any paper, patent, product or rule the records mention, copied and marked for the lawyer to verify.
- **Counsel's questions,** answered only from the records, where counsel sent a list.
- **Open facts:** the factual questions the records leave unanswered, each with who can answer it.

Then the lines counsel confirms and a check block, and after the brief, the list of missing records for the founder or engineering lead to collect. The founder or engineering lead checks the brief against the records and sends it to counsel.

## What has to change for your company

Your records are the heart of it. Decide where contributor records and the disclosure log live, who keeps them, and what counts as a record: notebook pages, commits, drawings, trial reports, slides, quotes. Agree the column headings once, so every brief reads them the same way. If you have never kept a disclosure log, the first brief will show how much is missing; that list is the start of one.

Name the patent counsel who receives each brief, and who covers when they are away. If counsel uses a disclosure form or a question list, use it as an input every time. Decide what confidentiality label your company puts on briefs; whether any further marking is needed is counsel's call, so a label that is itself a privilege marking is left off and the brief says so.

We sit with the people who build your products and the counsel who protects them, write down how inventions are really recorded, and set it up in the AI product your company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
