# IP ownership and licence question table

Before an agreement or statement of work is signed, IP counsel gets one table of the ownership, assignment, licence, background-IP and confidentiality clauses, each quoted with its section, set against the company's own IP positions and led by whether the assignment words counsel expects were found.

*Workflow template · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/ip-terms-question-table/)*

## The outcome

IP counsel opens one table and sees every clause the search finds on ownership, assignment, licence, background IP and confidentiality, in the agreement and each statement of work that goes with it, quoted in full with its document and section. Counsel confirms that nothing is missing. The first thing on the page is the assignment check: whether the words your positions file expects for an assignment appear anywhere at all. Under it, each clause is set beside the position it touches, the words counsel listed are marked found or not found, and every gap is already written as a question for counsel to answer.

This is preparation for a lawyer, and it stays preparation. It gives no legal advice. It never says who owns anything, never settles a clause whose meaning is open, and writes no replacement wording, not even for counsel to edit. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines, and it sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. Counsel decides what each clause means and how the company responds.

## Who starts the work, and when

A contracts manager or contracts administrator starts it when an agency, contractor, consultant, design, content or software agreement arrives with its statements of work, or a new statement of work arrives under an agreement already signed. It is also useful when counsel wants every IP clause of a signed agreement quoted in one place for a financing or acquisition file.

The table is addressed to the lawyer who decides: in-house IP counsel, the responsible attorney, or, where the company has no lawyer of its own, outside counsel by name. It is never addressed to the business as an answer, and it is not for the team that commissioned the work to act on.

## What they supply

- The agreement and every statement of work, schedule, order form and amendment that goes with it, in full.
- A short request: the counterparty, the type of agreement, the company's role in it (for example, commissioning the work), which version this is, and the name of the IP counsel who decides.
- The company's IP positions file, approved and dated by a lawyer: each position in the lawyer's words, the words that bring a clause under it, the words the lawyer expects to see and the words the lawyer wants pointed out, and which position is the assignment check.

The workflow template ships no positions and no law. Without an in-date positions file it still quotes and locates every clause and leaves every comparison to counsel.

## What comes out

One table, headed as a draft for the named counsel:

- **Assignment check:** the expected assignment words found or not found, document by document, with any flag words (the words counsel wants pointed out) quoted.
- **Clause table:** each clause quoted with its section, the words that put it there, the defined terms it uses and the positions it touches.
- **Positions:** for each position, which of counsel's words were found in the clauses that touch it, and where.
- **Links:** positions touched in more than one document, any clause using conflict or precedence words quoted and not applied, other clauses that point at the table, and IP mentions in warranties left for the general contract review.
- **Missing:** schedules, lists and other written terms the documents refer to that were not supplied, quoted with where they are referred to.
- **Counsel to confirm:** a numbered question for every point, including each name a clause uses that no document defines, each with a line for counsel's answer, and a closing question asking counsel whether any IP clause is missing.

The contracts manager checks every quotation against the documents and gives the table to counsel. Those are the only steps it names for anyone. It suggests nothing about contacting the other side or telling the business; asked for a verdict, wording, a case or what to do, it says only that this is for the named counsel and where the table puts the question. Nothing goes to the other side or to the business until counsel has answered.

## What has to change for your company

The positions file is yours, and it is the heart of this. Your lawyer decides which agreements and which company role it covers, writes each position, and chooses the words that bring a clause under each one, the words they expect to see and the words they want pointed out. Add your own agreements' terms, such as the name your suppliers give their pre-existing materials. Name who counsel is for each kind of agreement, and the outside counsel to use where you have no lawyer. Decide where counsel's answers are recorded and who tells the business the outcome.

We sit with your contracts staff and your counsel, write down how IP terms are really reviewed, and set it up in the AI product your company has approved: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Your company keeps control of how the work gets done.
