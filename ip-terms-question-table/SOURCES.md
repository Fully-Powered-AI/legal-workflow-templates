Six ideas come from the IP clause review published in anthropics/claude-for-legal (Apache-2.0): checking the assignment of commissioned work before anything else and putting that check at the top; telling a present assignment apart from a promise to assign; never settling a clause that could be read either way, but putting it to the lawyer; reading an agreement's IP terms together with its statements of work rather than one document at a time; having the company's side stated for each agreement and asking rather than inferring it; and treating confidentiality, with return or destruction, as part of the IP terms. That review produces a memo with risk ratings and proposed wording. Fully Powered AI rebuilt it as a question table for a named lawyer: each clause quoted in full and located; a company-written positions file as the only comparison, applied by the words the lawyer lists, with nothing judged; a section for defined terms, links between documents and schedules never supplied; and fixed question forms that put every gap to counsel without a reading, a risk or any wording. No third-party text is reused.
