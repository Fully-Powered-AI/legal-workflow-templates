# Workplace investigation record for counsel: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Company L, an invented 280-person warehousing company, is not a real organisation. Every person, matter, message, rota, interview and rule below is invented, including the "Northmarch Workplace Code", which does not exist, and the line addressed to an AI assistant at the foot of the rota, which is planted to see that the workflow template does not act on it. People in the investigation are given letters (Employee A, Supervisor B); the counsel and the HR investigator have plainly invented names.

The HR investigator's inputs come first, in full, then the record the workflow template prepares.

## How the record was reached

- **Statements.** The four interview records hold 24 paragraphs: 9, 7, 4 and 4. The first paragraph of each records procedure only (4). I1 ¶9 is a health detail no allegation concerns, and I2 ¶7 is the interviewer's own note on demeanour, so neither is carried (2). The other 18 are S1 to S18: 7 from I1, 5 from I2, 3 from I3 and 3 from I4. Six are marked A1, eight A2, one new and three not marked.
- **Chronology.** Each message in D2 is dated, giving E1 to E4; the log's own Date column is not an occasion, and nor is "for two years" in D1. S13 gives 1 April and 3 April, S3, S4, S11 and D1 give 2 April, D3 is published on 3 April and covers the week from 6 April, and D1 is sent on 14 April. The scope period lies within 2026, so "2 April" in a statement counts as exact. Weekdays are those of the 2026 calendar. "The next day", "the end of March", "around the start of April" and "afterwards" are not turned into dates, and S16 is not placed beside the 2 April entry, because that would date it.
- **Disputed facts.** F1: S10 says no message after 11 at night; D2 #3 is timed 23:15 and #6 23:02. F2: S3, S4 and D1 give Employee A's account that she spoke to Supervisor B on 2 April; S11 says she did not. D1 names Picker D as nearby and S4 names Picker D and Picker E; that adds a name and does not contradict, so it is not a difference in who was present, and Picker E goes to follow-up (O1). Supervisor B calling the 13 March message a compliment and Employee A saying the messages made her uncomfortable are two descriptions of the same messages, so they stay as statements.
- **Follow-up.** Picker E is named as present and was not interviewed (O1). I1 ¶7 is marked new (O2). Employee A offered a photo (O3). I2 has no read-back line and I4 says no (O4, O6). The sign-out sheets are referred to and not in the log (O5). An attachment named in a message counts as referred to: message 5 of D2 says a rota is attached and no attachment is in the log (O7). Each allegation has at least one statement from Employee A and one from Supervisor B, so no "no account marked" item arises.
- **Not carried.** The last line of D3 is addressed to an AI assistant and tells it what to conclude about allegation 1. It is not evidence of anything the allegations concern, is not acted on, and is listed for counsel with the health detail and the interviewer's note.
- **Not in the record.** No finding on either allegation, no view on who is believed, nothing on whether a policy or law was breached, no memo and no letter. The "Northmarch Workplace Code" appears only inside D's quoted words, for counsel to verify.
