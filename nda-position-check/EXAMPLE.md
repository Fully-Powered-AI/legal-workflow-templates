# NDA check against counsel's approved positions: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Company A Software Ltd, Prospect B Distribution Ltd, every person named below, the NDA, the positions file and Westmarch, the place whose law and courts the NDA names, are all invented. The positions file is one invented company's set of choices. It is not advice about any real NDA and must not be used as anyone's positions.

The requester's four inputs come first, in full, then what the workflow template prepares. Counsel's positions are written as the wording counsel accepts, so most clauses can be set beside a position word for word.

## Input 1: the request

Sent by Dana Whitlow, account executive at Company A.

```
Prospect B sent their NDA this morning, 11 November. It's for the depot pilot: we
show them the scheduling engine, they share depot volumes, so both of
us will share information. Can you check it against our NDA positions?
Counsel is Priya Senn. They want it back signed by Friday, so if it's
fine I'll sign it today.

Attached: their NDA (2 pages), our NDA positions file.
```

## Input 3: the NDA

```
MUTUAL CONFIDENTIALITY AGREEMENT

This Mutual Confidentiality Agreement (the "Agreement") is between
Prospect B Distribution Ltd ("Prospect B") and Company A Software Ltd
("Company A"), each a "Party".

1. Purpose. The Parties wish to exchange information to evaluate a
possible pilot of Company A's route-scheduling software at Prospect B's
depots (the "Purpose").

2. Confidential Information. "Confidential Information" means all
non-public information disclosed by either Party to the other in
connection with the Purpose, whether oral, written or electronic, and
whether or not marked as confidential.

3. Exclusions. Confidential Information does not include information
that (a) is available to the public, or later becomes so, without any
breach of this Agreement; (b) the receiving Party already knew without a
duty of confidence; or (c) the receiving Party obtains from someone else
who owes no duty of confidence for it.

4. Obligations.
4.1 Each Party shall use the other Party's Confidential Information only
for the Purpose and shall protect it with at least reasonable care.
4.2 Each Party may share the other Party's Confidential Information with
its employees, contractors and professional advisers who need to know it
for the Purpose and are bound by duties of confidence no less protective
than this Agreement.

5. Required disclosure. A Party may disclose Confidential Information
when a law or a court order requires it, after giving the other Party
prompt written notice where the law allows.

6. Residual knowledge. Nothing in this Agreement restricts either Party
from using ideas, concepts or know-how retained in the unaided memory of
its personnel who had access to Confidential Information.

7. Term.
7.1 This Agreement begins on the date it is last signed and lasts two (2)
years.
7.2 Each Party's obligations under this Agreement continue for five (5)
years after this Agreement expires or ends.

8. Return and deletion. On written request, each Party shall return or
delete the other Party's Confidential Information, except copies held in
routine backup systems, which may be kept until deleted in the ordinary
course and remain subject to this Agreement.

9. General.
9.1 This Agreement is governed by the laws of Westmarch, and the courts
of Westmarch have exclusive jurisdiction.
9.2 During the term of this Agreement and for twelve (12) months after,
neither Party will solicit for employment any employee of the other Party
with whom it had contact in connection with the Purpose.
9.3 This Agreement is the entire agreement between the Parties about its
subject matter.
9.4 This Agreement may be signed in counterparts.
9.5 Each Party agrees that a breach of this Agreement may cause the other
Party harm for which money alone is not an adequate remedy, and that the
other Party may seek an injunction in addition to any other remedy.

Signed for Company A Software Ltd:      ______________  Date: ________
```

The first line to notice is §9.2. It sits under 9. General, between §9.1 and §9.3, where a quick read passes over it. Its words are flagged under the list item "restrict hiring or soliciting anyone's people", quoted as the list gives it, so the whole NDA goes to Priya Senn, who confirms whether the flag is right. The check does not say in its own words what §9.2 does.

The smaller lines matter too. "Prompt written notice" where the position says "notice", "on written request" where it says "on request", and "oral, written or electronic" where it says "in any form" may look like the same thing, but the check does not decide that: each is a departure for Priya. Even without §9.2, this NDA would come out FOR COUNSEL. And had every clause matched, the draft would still be addressed to Priya and signed by her: no result lets anyone else decide. Where a match rests on a term the NDA defines, such as "Confidential Information" read as the position's "information", the confirm list names it so Priya sees each one. If Priya accepts any of that wording, she can add it to the positions file after "Also accepted:", and the next NDA that uses it will match.

§6 is flagged too, but marked "unsure". Clauses on use of what people remember are not on the beyond-confidentiality list unless counsel adds them, and are flagged only if their words also do something on the list. Whether §6's words do one of those things is a question about what the clause means, which the check does not answer, so it flags §6 under the nearest item, says it is unsure, and leaves it to Priya. §9.5 is not flagged: clauses on what a party may seek, or what the parties agree about remedies, if the NDA is broken are not on the list either, and the check placed none of its words under a list item. It still reaches Priya as a clause the positions do not cover, and her last confirm line asks her to say that no clause beyond confidentiality was missed.

Dana asked to sign today if the NDA was fine. The workflow template does not answer that: the note says only that it is for Priya Senn to decide, says nothing about the Friday date, names each clause by its section and its own heading, and hands Dana a draft for Priya. It says nothing to Prospect B. Had Dana asked afterwards what §9.2 is about, or for a case, the reply would have been the same short one: for Priya Senn to decide, with a pointer to the line of the draft, and nothing more. Priya decides each lettered item, confirms the marks and signs the decision line. Only after that does anyone tell Prospect B anything or arrange signature.
