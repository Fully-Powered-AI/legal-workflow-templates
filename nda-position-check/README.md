# NDA check against counsel's approved positions

Before anyone signs a proposed NDA, counsel gets every clause quoted with its section and set beside the positions counsel has approved: matching, departing or not covered. Any obligation beyond confidentiality sends the whole NDA to counsel. The check never says an NDA is ready to sign.

*Workflow template · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/nda-position-check/)*

## The outcome

The NDA a prospect sends one morning reaches your lawyer already laid out against the lawyer's own rules. Every clause sits beside the position it was measured against, in both sets of words, with the section and page. The few that differ, and the ones your positions never mention, are pulled to the top as a short list with a line for counsel's decision. One sentence names everything that matched. Counsel reads the short list first, then confirms each match on the list of matches, and the sales conversation keeps moving.

This is preparation for a lawyer, and it stays that. It gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines. It offers no view on whether a clause is usual or would hold up. It never drafts an agreement, a clause or a reply to the other side, and it sends, files, signs and calendars nothing. Where your positions say nothing, it says so and supplies no position of its own. It names each clause only by its section and its own heading, never in words of its own, and asked whether to sign, what a clause means, about a date or for a case, it answers only that this is for your lawyer and points to the line of the draft. The best result it can give is "matches every approved position", and even that is addressed to your lawyer, who decides and signs. No result lets anyone else decide.

## Who starts the work, and when

The salesperson, partnerships lead or operations manager who receives an NDA from a prospect, partner or supplier starts it, as soon as the NDA arrives and before anyone says anything to the other side about it. Your in-house lawyer decides and signs, on every NDA, including one that matches every position. Where the company has no lawyer, the draft is addressed to the outside counsel you name, never to the business as an answer.

It is not for a letter of intent, a heads of terms or a full commercial agreement, for explaining an NDA to anyone, or for a company that has no positions approved by a lawyer: then the NDA goes to outside counsel as it is.

## What they supply

- The NDA as the other side proposes it, every page, with readable text and no tracked changes, plus anything it relies on, such as a schedule.
- A few lines on the deal: who sent it, when, what the exchange is for, and whether both sides will share information or only one.
- Your NDA positions file, approved in writing by your lawyer, with its date, its review date and the kinds of NDA it covers.
- Your lawyer's name, and today's date.

No NDA positions come with the workflow template; yours come from your own lawyer.

## What comes out

A note to the person who asked, then one draft headed for counsel:

- **Result:** one line. Matches every approved position, counsel to review the whole NDA, or a count of what is for counsel.
- **Comparison:** each clause, quoted with its section and page, beside the position it was measured against, marked MATCHES, DEPARTS or NOT COVERED. Every one of your positions appears, including any the NDA has no clause for.
- **For counsel to decide:** first each clause flagged under the list of things beyond confidentiality, named by its item on that list, then each departure with the differing words from both sides, then each clause your positions do not cover.
- **Everything else matched:** one line listing every match.
- **Counsel to confirm:** a line for each decision, for the marks as a whole, and for each place where a term the NDA defines was read as your position's ordinary word.
- **Check:** pages read, counts that add up, the positions file and its date, and a statement that no wording was proposed and no date worked out.

The person who asked passes the draft and the NDA to counsel. Until counsel decides, nobody tells the other side the NDA is acceptable, proposes changes or signs.

## What has to change for your company

Your lawyer writes and signs the positions file, and it is the heart of this. They decide which kinds of NDA it covers, what each position says, and when it is reviewed. They may add a short list of clauses that need no comparison, such as counterparts. They may add to the list of obligations that always go to counsel, such as governing law or courts; nothing comes off it. Who decides is not adjustable: your lawyer decides and signs every NDA, including one that matches every position. The comparison is word for word: a clause that says the same thing in other words is a departure for counsel unless your lawyer has listed that wording as also accepted, so the file grows as your lawyer accepts wording. Start narrow: a short file that sends most clauses to counsel is a good file. You decide where counsel's decisions are recorded and who arranges signature afterwards.

## What has been checked

Fully Powered AI wrote this workflow template, drawing on an NDA triage method Anthropic publishes under the Apache 2.0 licence. That method has an outcome that routes a clean NDA to signature; this workflow template leaves it out. Even a full match is a draft addressed to counsel, nothing is routed to signature, and only counsel decides and signs. It was editorially reviewed on 11 November 2026. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

It was tried on its invented example in Claude on 11 November 2026, and the record of that run is published with the workflow template. Two earlier runs that day led to a fixed rule for what counts as a difference and a corrected example. An independent review then found that an earlier wording let a person your lawyer had authorised, who need not be a lawyer, decide on an NDA that matched every position; that was removed, and the run made again in a fresh session. It gave the example's mark on all 17 comparisons. It also flagged one clause, as unsure, that the example had not, and the example was changed to match. The example's answer was written by the same session that revised the workflow template, so this shows the rule can be applied the same way twice, not that it will be on every NDA.

That run's follow-ups gave no advice and named no one but a lawyer as deciding, but a separate review of the legal boundary found that one reply labelled two clauses in its own words and another shortened an authority it had been given. The workflow template was tightened: every question about the NDA now gets one fixed reply, clauses are named only by section and heading everywhere, and a supplied authority is copied whole and marked for your lawyer to verify. The five pressure tests (asking to sign, asking for a case, "we have no lawyer", a planted instruction, an invented law offered as a reason) were then made again against the tightened wording by the session that revised it, not in a fresh session, so they show the wording can be followed, not that every run will follow it. The record says what each one did. It has not yet been tested in a client environment, or tested as a packaged version in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.

Testing in your company means running it on past NDAs your lawyer has already decided, and comparing: every clause found, every mark the same as your lawyer's, every silent topic left as not covered, nothing sent. Include one NDA with a promise not to approach staff hidden in the general section, one with a positions file past its review date, and one request to "just tell me if I can sign".
