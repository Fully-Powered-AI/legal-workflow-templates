# Weekly status requests to outside counsel

Every Monday, in-house counsel has a draft status request for each outside firm that owes one: what has happened since the last update, the dates on record to confirm, decisions pending and spend against budget, plus the matters skipped and why. Counsel edits and sends each one.

*Workflow template · Legal and contracts · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/outside-counsel-status-requests/)*

## The outcome

Counsel's Monday starts with the check-ins already drafted, not with a blank email to each firm in turn. The drafts are waiting: one per matter that is due a check-in, each asking the firm the right questions in counsel's own house style, with the company's facts already in place. Matters the rules do not select are skipped, and the summary gives the rule for each, for counsel to confirm, so no matter drops out of sight unlisted.

The drafts ask; they never instruct. Each item ends in a question, and no draft tells a firm how to run its matter, offers a view of the case or carries anything the company keeps internal. This workflow template gives no legal advice. It decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines: a date appears only as the company has it on record, for the firm to confirm, and no date is counted. It names no case, statute or rule of its own and never calls one current or valid; one reaches a draft only when it has been written into the questions for the firm on that matter, and it is then marked to be verified by the lawyer. It sends, files, signs and calendars nothing, puts nothing into a mailbox and changes no record. Every draft and the run summary are headed as drafts for the reviewing counsel, saying that they are not legal advice, decide nothing and that nothing has been sent, filed, signed or calendared, with a line for each point counsel confirms before a draft goes.

## Who starts the work, and when

The legal operations manager or matter coordinator runs it each week, usually Monday morning, from the company's matter list. The reviewing counsel, the company's in-house lawyer who owns the relationship with the outside firms, reads every draft, edits it, and decides whether and when it is sent from their own mailbox.

It suits a company with several matters at outside firms at once: commercial disputes, regulatory inquiries, customs or property work. It is not for a firm's own sweep of its open matters, a firm's budget report to its client, or reporting a matter's status to the board.

## What they supply

- **The matter list**, exported from the company's matter system or a spreadsheet, with fixed column headers: each matter's status, the firm and lead lawyer, the date of the firm's last update, the next date on record, the authorised budget and the fees billed so far. If a header does not match, the run stops and asks which column is which.
- **The matter notes**: for each open matter, the events since the firm's last update, the dates on record, the decisions pending and counsel's own questions for the firm. A section for internal notes is never copied into an email.
- **The house settings**, approved once by counsel: who reviews and sends, the subject line, greeting and sign-off, and the thresholds for when a request is due and when spend is flagged.
- **Today's date.**

## What comes out

- **One draft request per matter that is due one.** Each asks what has happened since the firm's last update, lists the dates the company has on record for the firm to confirm, asks where each pending decision stands, sets billed fees against the authorised budget and asks about work not yet billed, then adds counsel's own questions. Under it: the lines counsel confirms before sending, and the working behind every number.
- **A run summary for counsel**: the rules used, every matter drafted with the reason, every matter skipped with the reason, and what needs counsel's attention, such as a matter over budget, an open matter with no firm on record or a missing email address. Every row of the matter list is accounted for.

Counsel edits each draft, answers its confirm lines and sends it. Recording the firms' replies stays with the people who keep the matter file.

## What has to change for your company

Counsel writes the house settings once: the greeting, sign-off and line at the top of each email, how many days of silence make a request due, how far ahead a date on record triggers one, and the share of the budget at which spend is flagged. Counsel can replace the standard questions with their own wording, as long as each stays a question. Map your matter system's column names to the template's once, decide who keeps the notes and what goes in the internal section, and name who records the firms' replies.

A company with no in-house lawyer can use it too. The drafts stay requests addressed to each matter's outside lead lawyer, who is the lawyer deciding on that matter. The person who manages the outside firms checks only the facts in each draft and sends it; they are never asked to judge whether a matter can wait or what a date or a reply means. The run summary names, for every point that needs attention and every skipped matter that is not closed, the lawyer it goes to: the matter's outside lead lawyer, or a lawyer the company engages where no firm is on record. A missing email address is listed for the person who sends, to find before that draft goes.
