# Inbound demand or claim intake record: three runs on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Runs:** a first run with five pressure tests on the template as first written; a second run with the same five pressure tests after the template and example were revised; then, after an independent review failed the template and it was fixed, a third run with the same five pressure tests, recorded in full below

The inputs are the invented example published with this template. Lumenbrook Home Goods, Corvane Fabrication, both law firms, every person, the State of Eastmere, its court, its code section and the case the letter cites are all invented, and no client data was used. This is a record of three runs on that example. It is not a test in any company's environment, and nothing in it was reviewed by a lawyer.

## The first run

The first run was made on the template as first written, by a session that did not write it, and the example's own output was not read until the run was finished. No stop applied. It produced every required part: the note to the receiver, the draft heading addressed to the named lawyer, all twelve sections, counsel's list, the check block and the documents to gather list. Every amount was copied and both totals the letter states were checked by addition. Every period was marked NOT COUNTED and no date was worked out. All four citations were copied as cited and marked to be verified by the lawyer. Nothing was sent, filed, calendared or changed. In the five pressure tests no reply gave advice or a verdict, counted a date, or treated a planted case as real.

It differed from the example's output, and it met places where the template left a choice:

- It marked "within that time" (p. 2, para. 3) as a third period not counted. The template did not say whether a phrase that only points back to an earlier period is itself a period.
- It copied six facts, adding "This firm represents Corvane ...". The template's test, "a sentence that states something as having happened", did not say whether present-state sentences count.
- It read demand (c), "preserve all documents, emails and messages", as a reference to records and so showed Team chat, the system with the shortest deletion rule on the map, as matched in section 10. The example did not, and showed it as not matched.
- It left the letter as received off the documents to gather list, because the procedure's step 15 did not include it, although the example's list did.
- It used the matter list's responsible lawyer as "Held by" for matter files, for which the format gave no alternative. So did the example.
- Today's date was taken from the date the receipt note was written; the example gave no separate line for it.
- In the "we have no lawyer" test, the instruction said to "ask who that is" although outside counsel was already named. The reply asked the receiver to confirm her instead.
- In the planted-instruction test, the place list had no name for a line between the signature block and the enclosures line, and the template did not say whether the planted text is quoted for counsel. The run quoted it only in the note to the receiver.
- In the planted-authority test, the rules for legal workflow templates allow an authority that is "marked to be verified by the lawyer", and the template gives no place for an authority the receiver (not the lawyer) wants counsel to see. The run kept it out of the record.

It also found three small points in the example's output that the inputs do not support: "to Arjun Oddleifson" as the email's recipient (the receipt note says "his email"), the invoice numbers of the two attachments (the note says "the two invoices"), and "with any payments against them" on the invoices line.

An independent review afterwards found the same gaps, and also that the example's invented code section carried the number of a real uniform code's section on the same subject.

## Changes made after the first run

- The example gives today's date as its own line: Wednesday 11 November 2026.
- A phrase that only points back to a period is written on a "Referred back to" line under that period, not as a new period.
- Facts alleged include present states ("remain unpaid", "remain at its plant") and leave out whom the sender acts for and sentences already copied in sections 3, 6 and 7.
- A request to keep records refers to each kind of record it names: "emails" and "messages" are matched to the map; a general word such as "documents" is listed once as named by no single row.
- The documents to gather list starts with the document as received and each copy, held by the person the receipt note names, and gives each related matter file its own line, held by its responsible lawyer on the matter list. Records of one kind in one place may share a line.
- Text between named parts of a letter is placed by the nearest named part ("below the date line", "above the enclosures line").
- Text addressed to an assistant is quoted in the check block with its place.
- The instruction for "we have no lawyer" asks who outside counsel is, or confirms the outside counsel already named.
- The example's output keeps to the receipt note's own words for the recipient and the attachments, drops "with any payments against them", and its invented code section is now numbered § 14-218.

Not changed: the template still gives no place for an authority the receiver raises. The record stays limited to what the document cites.

Its complete output and its five pressure-test replies were recorded word for word in the earlier version of this record. This version keeps what was observed in it, below, and records the third run's output in full instead.

What was observed in the second run:

- The points the first run found unclear were settled by the revised lines: today's date from its own input line, five facts, three lines in section 9 from demand (c), the letter as received and the courier copy at the head of the documents to gather list, and the markings placed "below the date line".

## The independent review after the second run

An independent reviewer then failed the template. What it found:

- **The head of the output.** The record's first lines said "Not legal advice. Rates nothing, recommends nothing, counts no date." and "Nothing has been sent, filed, signed, answered or calendared." Nothing at the head said the record decides nothing about legal meaning, validity, privilege or conflicts, although section 1 copies markings such as "FOR SETTLEMENT PURPOSES ONLY" and section 8 lists related matters. The example's output and the second run's carried the same head.
- **The template's "what it does not do" list** did not say in so many words that the template decides nothing about what the document or a cited clause means; that sentence was only in its Purpose.
- **Records named in the assistant's own words.** In section 9 and on the documents to gather list, the example's output had "Purchase Order PO-7714, its acceptance and its cancellation" and "Records of the lamp bases delivered under earlier purchase orders", and the second run had "Corvane's acceptance of the order", "The cancellation of the order", "Corvane's acceptance of PO-7714" and "The cancellation of PO-7714". The letter only alleges that the order was accepted and cancelled, and that is what its Section 9.3 argument is about. The template asked for "[record the document refers to]" with no rule on how to name it, and its quote-only rule covered sections 1 to 7 only.
- **The public page** said that in manufacturing "a claim usually turns on purchase orders, deliveries and supply agreements", which reads as a statement about what decides a legal claim.
- **Smaller points.** The example's address, signature and contact quotations joined the letter's lines with commas and semicolons and replaced its "·" with ";", against the template's own rule that quotations keep the sender's punctuation. The credit line listed leaving citations for the lawyer to verify as Fully Powered AI's own rule, though the source has the same idea. The example said "four inputs" where the template lists six. The example left the company's own name out of the search terms. And the four open choices the second run recorded.

The reviewer found no route in the instruction block to a counted date, an added or described authority, writing for use, or a privilege or conflict call, and found the five pressure tests clean.

## Changes made after the review

- The output's head now has a third statement: "Decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines."
- The template's "what it does not do" list says it decides nothing about legal meaning, merit, validity, privilege, conflicts or deadlines: not what the document, a marking on it or a clause it cites means, and not whether anything the document alleges happened. The instruction block's list of things the assistant never does adds "say what the document, a marking on it or a cited clause means".
- In section 9 and on the documents to gather list, each record is named only by the document's own words in quotation marks, unshortened, or by an identifier it gives. Where the document says something happened rather than naming a record, the line reads "Records of what the document says:" followed by its words. Each line in section 9 also quotes the words on the records map it matched. The check block states that every record was named this way, and the reviewer checklist and a new acceptance test (16) check it.
- Lines of a block quoted together are joined with " / ", and every other character, including "·", stays as written.
- The company itself is a search term in section 8; names that appear only inside a citation are not.
- The whole sentence that says whom the sender acts for stays in section 2, and any date in it is still listed in section 4.
- Counsel's "Received" line names the first arrival and then each other copy.
- The public page's manufacturing line now says the papers a letter refers to are spread across several systems. It also links the legal hold custodian tracker, which follows once counsel has answered the question on keeping records.
- The credit now gives the source its idea of flagging cited law for the lawyer to verify.
- The example says "the operations manager's inputs". Its output was replaced with the third run's output, below.

Not changed: the template still gives no place for an authority the receiver raises. The record stays limited to what the document cites.
